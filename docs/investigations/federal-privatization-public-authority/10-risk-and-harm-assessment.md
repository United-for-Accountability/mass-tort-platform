# Risk and Harm Assessment

**Investigation:** Federal Privatization, Private Control, and the Transfer of Public Authority
**Date:** November 18, 2026

---

> This assessment distinguishes between documented harms (things that have happened and are recorded) and risks under investigation (things that might happen or might have happened but have not been proven). Only documented harms form the basis for legal claims. Risks are recorded to guide the investigation.

---

## 1. Documented harms

### 1.1 November 13, 2026 nationwide ground stop

**What is documented:** The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on November 13, 2026. Air travel was disrupted. The FAA's preliminary explanation attributes the event to legacy equipment instability when data flooded the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure network, affecting 17 of 22 en route centers. (E-003)

**What is not documented:** The root cause. Whether any contractor action or decision contributed to the failure. Whether the modernization program or the contract transition contributed. Whether any person suffered a legally cognizable injury traceable to a specific contractor decision.

**Current status:** Documented service disruption. Root cause unresolved.

### 1.2 GAO cost and schedule findings

**What is documented:** GAO found in September 2026 that the Brand New ATC System program lacked a reliable lifecycle cost estimate and an integrated master schedule. (E-004)

**What is not documented:** Whether these deficiencies caused specific harm to any identified person. Whether they represent waste in the legal sense.

**Current status:** Documented management deficiency. Harm to specific persons not established.

## 2. Risks under investigation

The following risks have been identified based on the structure of the contracts and programs under review. They are not documented harms unless proven. They are recorded to guide the investigation.

### 2.1 Operational dependence / inability to replace a contractor

**Description:** If the government depends on a single contractor for critical infrastructure and cannot replace that contractor without losing service, the government's practical ability to exercise oversight and enforce accountability is compromised.

**What would need to be established:** That the government lacks the technical capability, data rights, and transition provisions to replace the contractor without loss of service; that this dependence results from contractual choices rather than unavoidable technical realities.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires contract review.

### 2.2 Vendor lock-in and long-term cost increase

**Description:** Proprietary standards, formats, or interfaces may prevent the government from switching contractors or using competitive pressure to control costs.

**What would need to be established:** Specific contract provisions creating lock-in; evidence that the lock-in results in higher costs than a competitive market would produce.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires contract review.

### 2.3 Improper exercise of governmental authority

**Description:** A contractor employee makes final, binding decisions that the law requires to be made by federal officials.

**What would need to be established:** Specific decisions with governmental effect; evidence that the decisions were made without meaningful federal review; a legal basis for challenging the arrangement.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires contract review and evidence of specific decisions.

### 2.4 Concentration of control across essential services

**Description:** The same ultimate owners control critical infrastructure across multiple federal agencies, creating systemic risk if those owners face financial distress, change strategy, or are subject to foreign influence.

**What would need to be established:** Beneficial ownership of contractors across agencies; evidence of common control; evidence that the concentration creates specific, cognizable risk.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires ownership investigation.

### 2.5 Loss of public access to records held by contractors

**Description:** Records about the operation of essential public services are held by private contractors and are not accessible under FOIA.

**What would need to be established:** That the specific records are not agency records (which would be subject to FOIA) but are held exclusively by the contractor; that the government does not have adequate data rights to access and produce them.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires contract review and FOIA testing.

### 2.6 Security and continuity failures

**Description:** Contractor-run networks and systems may have security vulnerabilities or continuity gaps that the government cannot independently assess or remedy.

**What would need to be established:** Specific vulnerabilities or gaps; evidence that the government lacks the access or authority to assess and remedy them; evidence that any failure caused or could cause a cognizable harm.

**Current status:** Unproven. Requires specialized technical review beyond this investigation's current scope.

## 3. Harm assessment framework for potential plaintiffs

Before any legal action can be considered, the investigation must identify:

| Element | Description | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete injury | An actual, particularized harm (not speculative or generalized) | Not yet identified |
| Causation | The harm is fairly traceable to a specific government action or contractor decision | Not yet established |
| Legal basis | The harm results from a violation of a recognized legal right | Not yet established |
| Redressability | A court could remedy the harm | Not yet established |
| Timing | The harm is not barred by a statute of limitations | Not yet assessed |

As of November 18, 2026, no plaintiff with a concrete, traceable, legally cognizable injury has been identified.
