# Methodologies

**Investigation:** Federal Privatization, Private Control, and the Transfer of Public Authority
**Date:** November 18, 2026

---

## Section 1: Contractor and beneficial-ownership investigation methodology

### 1.1 Purpose

To identify who ultimately owns and controls the contractors performing essential functions under federal contracts, using lawful public records, and to record ownership and control facts transparently without inferring coordination, conspiracy, or wrongdoing from ownership alone.

### 1.2 Why ownership matters

A government that depends operationally on a contractor it cannot replace without losing service has less accountability than one that can. If the same ultimate owners control multiple essential-function contractors across multiple agencies, the concentration matters for continuity and accountability — regardless of whether it is unlawful. Ownership concentration is recorded as a fact; it is not treated as evidence of misconduct unless specific evidence of misconduct is found.

### 1.3 Sources

**Primary sources (in priority order):**
1. SEC filings (10-K, DEF 14A, 8-K) — for publicly traded companies or companies with public debt obligations
2. State corporate registration records — for entity structure and registered agents
3. SAM.gov — for contractor registration, entity hierarchy, and CAGE codes
4. USAspending.gov — for contract awards and modification history
5. Federal contract databases — for contract type, value, and period
6. FEC filings — for political contributions that may be relevant to procurement influence questions
7. Congressional testimony and committee reports

**Secondary sources:**
- Press releases and company websites
- Trade publication coverage
- Investigative journalism from credentialed outlets

**Not treated as evidence:**
- Social media posts
- Anonymous tips without corroborating records
- Other organizations' allegations without cited primary sources

### 1.4 Beneficial ownership limitation

As of August 2026, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) has permanently exempted U.S. companies and U.S. persons from Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting (E-019). This eliminates one avenue for identifying the ultimate beneficial owners of private companies (such as private-equity-backed contractors). Investigation will rely on:
- Private equity fund SEC filings (Form ADV, Form PF where applicable)
- Portfolio company disclosures in PE fund materials
- Merger filing disclosures (HSR Act)
- Press coverage of acquisitions
- Congressional or IG investigation records

### 1.5 Ownership recording protocol

| Element | How recorded |
| --- | --- |
| Direct ownership | Record legal owner, percentage, and source |
| Indirect / beneficial ownership | Record chain to the extent public records allow; note limits |
| Private equity sponsorship | Record fund name, manager, and any disclosed limited partners |
| Institutional investment | Record based on 13F or equivalent filings; note as passive unless specific control rights are documented |
| Common institutional ownership | Record without inferring coordination; note as a pattern of potential interest |
| Foreign ownership | Record any disclosed foreign ownership or control; flag for national-security analysis |

### 1.6 Control vs. ownership

Ownership is not the same as operational control, and neither is the same as decision authority. For each contractor, the investigation separately records:
- Who owns the entity (beneficial ownership to the extent determinable)
- Who has contractual control (board rights, approval rights under contracts, termination rights)
- Who has operational control (who makes day-to-day operational decisions)
- Who has decision authority over governmental functions (who makes decisions that bind the public)

Only the last question has direct legal significance for privatization and nondelegation analysis.

## Section 2: Federal agency contracting review methodology

### 2.1 Purpose

To review federal contracts for provisions that may transfer governmental authority, create operational dependency, or raise accountability concerns, using the evidence register, FOIA records, and public contract databases.

### 2.2 Document hierarchy

| Priority | Document type | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Actual contract and all exhibits | Definitive; controls |
| 2 | FOIA-produced agency records | Official; authenticated |
| 3 | GAO or IG reports | Authoritative secondary analysis |
| 4 | Congressional testimony | Official; may characterize contract terms |
| 5 | SAM.gov and USAspending.gov data | Public award data; not contract text |
| 6 | Trade press | Useful for identifying contracts; not for terms |

### 2.3 For each contract reviewed, the following questions are asked

**Authority and scope:**
1. What specific tasks or functions are delegated to the contractor?
2. What decisions is the contractor authorized to make without FAA or agency approval?
3. What decisions require agency approval?
4. Who has final authority over design acceptance, system certification, and operational readiness?
5. Does the contract describe any function as inherently governmental, or does it address the inherently governmental function limitation?

**Ownership:**
6. Who owns the equipment provided under the contract?
7. Who owns the software and what are the license terms?
8. Who owns the data generated under the contract?
9. What are the government's data rights (access, export, use after termination)?

**Continuity and dependency:**
10. What are the termination provisions, and can the government terminate for convenience?
11. Are there transition or step-in provisions that allow the government to take over operations?
12. Does the contract create lock-in through proprietary standards, formats, or interfaces?
13. What is the contract's period of performance and option structure?

**Oversight:**
14. What audit rights does the government retain?
15. Are there performance reporting requirements?
16. What records must the contractor preserve, and in what format?
17. Are there any provisions limiting the government's ability to inspect contractor facilities or records?

**Conflicts:**
18. Are there organizational conflict-of-interest provisions?
19. Does the contractor have related business interests that could affect performance?

### 2.4 Classification of arrangements

After reviewing each contract, the arrangement is classified using the taxonomy on the public case page:

| Classification | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary procurement | Government buys goods or routine services; keeps all decisions |
| Contractor-operated support | Contractor staff do support work under federal direction |
| Government-owned, contractor-operated | Government owns the asset; contractor runs it |
| Contractor-owned, contractor-operated | Contractor owns and runs infrastructure the essential function depends on |
| Delegation of authority | Private party makes or effectively makes binding public decisions |
| Full privatization | Responsibility moves to a non-federal entity |

### 2.5 Cross-agency pattern analysis

After reviewing contracts for individual agencies, patterns across agencies will be noted. A pattern (e.g., the same contractor holding critical infrastructure roles across multiple agencies) is recorded as a fact. It does not by itself establish unlawful coordination, intent, or harm.

Criteria for flagging a cross-agency pattern:
- Same ultimate owner holds critical infrastructure across multiple agencies
- Same contractor holds both the integrator and the network role (vertical concentration)
- Contract terms across agencies are substantially identical in ways that limit government switching ability
- Common waiver of data rights or step-in provisions across agencies

### 2.6 Source authentication

Every record in the evidence register is authenticated by:
1. Retrieving from the original issuer's website (government .gov domain or officially designated repository)
2. Recording the URL, date accessed, and file hash
3. Preserving a copy in the investigation file

Records that cannot be authenticated are labeled Unverified and are not used as the sole basis for any factual claim.
