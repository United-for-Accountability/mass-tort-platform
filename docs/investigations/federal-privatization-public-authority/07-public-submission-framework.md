# Public Submission Framework

**Investigation:** Federal Privatization, Private Control, and the Transfer of Public Authority
**Date:** November 18, 2026

---

## 1. Purpose of public submissions

Public submissions allow federal employees, contractor employees, affected members of the public, labor representatives, researchers, and others to provide documented information that may help the investigation identify specific records, contracts, or decisions to review. Every submission is treated as a lead to be checked against public records — not as a verified allegation.

## 2. What we are asking for

The most useful submissions are specific:

- A specific agency, office, program, or contract
- A specific date or range of dates
- A specific decision, change, or event that the submitter witnessed or documented
- Public records, contract numbers, or report names that could confirm the information
- Any Inspector General, Office of Special Counsel, congressional, agency, or union reports that have already been filed

General concerns about privatization, without specific supporting facts, are noted but are not actionable leads.

## 3. What we cannot accept

Submitters must not include:

- Classified or national-security information
- Records they were not lawfully allowed to take or share
- Passwords, credentials, or access tokens
- Other people's private personal, medical, or financial information, Social Security numbers, or account numbers
- Material covered by a court order or a nondisclosure obligation the submitter has not lawfully cleared

A submission that includes any of these materials will not be used and will be deleted.

## 4. What we cannot promise

United for Accountability cannot:

- Promise anonymity. This submission platform is not anonymous. We ask for contact information and retain it.
- Grant whistleblower protection. Legal protection for disclosures generally depends on making them through protected channels: an agency's Office of Inspector General (listed at oversight.gov), the U.S. Office of Special Counsel (osc.gov), or Congress.
- Grant immunity. Submitting to this investigation does not provide legal protection.
- Create an attorney-client relationship. Submitting does not establish any privilege.

## 5. Protected channels for employees

If a submitter is a federal or contractor employee and is concerned about retaliation:

- **Federal employees** may have whistleblower protection under the Whistleblower Protection Act (5 U.S.C. §§ 2302, 1221) for disclosures to the Office of Special Counsel, Inspectors General, or Congress.
- **Contractor employees** may have protection under 41 U.S.C. § 4712 for disclosures to Inspectors General, Members of Congress, or the Department of Justice.
- Neither protection applies to disclosures made to private organizations like United for Accountability.
- Employees should consider speaking with an attorney before sharing information they obtained at work.

## 6. Privacy handling

Submissions are handled as follows:

- Contact information is used only by authorized United for Accountability reviewers.
- We do not sell submissions.
- Nothing is published unless the submitter checks the public-use permission box, and even then contact information is removed.
- Submissions are deleted or anonymized when no longer needed for the investigation.

## 7. How submissions are used

1. Each submission is logged with its timestamp and submission category.
2. It is reviewed against existing public records and the evidence register.
3. If the submission identifies a specific, verifiable record, that record is added to the FOIA queue or evidence register.
4. The submission itself is treated as a lead, not as evidence. No factual claim on the public page or in the investigation file is based solely on a public submission without corroborating public records.

## 8. Submission categories

The form asks submitters to identify the category or categories that apply:

1. Federal employee experience
2. Contractor employee experience
3. Procurement or contracting concern
4. Public service disruption
5. Public infrastructure ownership
6. Corporate ownership information
7. Financial or billing concern
8. Agency transparency concern
9. Legal or constitutional concern
10. Other documented evidence

## 9. High-value submission types

The following types of information are most useful:

| Information type | Why useful |
| --- | --- |
| Contract numbers or modification numbers | Allows targeted FOIA requests |
| Names of specific decisions or approvals | Allows review of decision records |
| Dates of specific events | Allows correlation with public records |
| Names of programs or systems | Allows procurement database searches |
| IG or OSC report numbers | Allows retrieval of existing oversight findings |
| GAO engagement numbers | Allows retrieval of existing oversight findings |
| Congressional correspondence | May be in public record |
| Press release or announcement references | Allows cross-reference with public statements |
