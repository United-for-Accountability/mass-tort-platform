## 1. Why FAA is the first pilot

Air traffic control is the first subject because it has identifiable, concurrent public records: a large, recently funded modernization program; a named prime integrator; contractor-run telecommunications networks; a long-standing contract tower program; recent GAO and Inspector General findings; a nationwide ground stop with ongoing root-cause analysis; and a $50.1 billion congressional funding request.

The combination makes it possible to build a factual record from public sources and to identify specific documents to request.

## 5. The November 13, 2026 ground stop

The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on November 13, 2026. The FAA's public explanation (E-003) states that legacy equipment became unstable when data flooded the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure network, affecting 17 of 22 en route centers. The root-cause investigation was ongoing as of November 15, 2026.

**What is established:** A nationwide ground stop occurred and disrupted air travel. The FAA has stated a preliminary technical cause.

**What is not established:** Whether the contractor-run network contributed to or caused the instability. Whether any design or maintenance decision by a contractor preceded the failure. Whether the ground stop was related to the modernization program or the contract transition. These questions are open and require the root-cause report and contract records.

## 6. The $50.1 billion request

On November 17, 2026, the Secretary of Transportation, the OMB Director, and the FAA Administrator sent a joint letter to the House and Senate Appropriations Committees requesting $50.1 billion for air traffic control modernization and airport infrastructure.

The letter specifies (from E-001):
- Up to 140 new FAA-owned control towers
- A common automation platform for controllers
- Controller workstations
- Communication infrastructure upgrades
- Airport runway safety improvements

**Ownership implication:** The request specifically funds FAA-owned towers, which is consistent with continued federal ownership of the physical plant. The request does not address software ownership, data rights, or operational control under the integrator contract.

## 7. Project 2025 proposals

The Heritage Foundation's Mandate for Leadership 2025 (Chapter 19) recommends separating the FAA from DOT, or at minimum separating the Air Traffic Organization from the FAA, and funding air traffic services through user fees, operating the FAA more like a business. It describes these as changes requiring legislation.

**Status: Verified as proposals. No implementing action has been identified.**

These proposals are recorded because they represent a stated policy direction that this investigation will monitor for implementation. A proposal is not an implemented change. Any characterization of Project 2025 recommendations as already implemented would be incorrect based on the current record.

## 8. GAO-26-107992 findings

In September 2026, GAO reported (E-004) that the Brand New Air Traffic Control System program:

1. Lacked a reliable lifecycle cost estimate.
2. Lacked an integrated master schedule.

**Status: Verified.** These are cost and schedule management findings. GAO did not find that the program was unlawful, that the contractor was exercising governmental authority, or that any privatization had occurred.

## 9. Contract tower program

The FAA has contracted out the staffing of some control towers since 1982. Congress authorizes and funds the program. In May 2026, the FAA began a pilot moving selected high-activity contract towers into FAA operation (E-016).

**Status: Verified.** The contract tower program is Congress-authorized and long-standing. The May 2026 pilot moves toward more federal operation, not less.

**Inherently governmental question.** Air traffic control clearances are exercises of federal airspace authority. The contract tower program has operated for over 40 years under congressional authorization, suggesting Congress has implicitly determined this arrangement is lawful. However, the investigation will confirm whether the program's scope has changed and whether the specific functions performed by contract tower controllers remain within the lawful scope of the program.

## 11. Counterarguments

1. The FAA retains statutory authority over the airspace under 49 U.S.C. § 40103. This authority has not been transferred by any action identified in the record.
2. The funding request specifically funds FAA-owned towers, which is not privatization.
3. The May 2026 contract tower conversion pilot moves towers toward federal operation, cutting against the privatization hypothesis.
4. Project 2025 recommendations require legislation, and no implementing legislation has been identified.
5. The contract tower program has operated lawfully for over 40 years under congressional authorization.
6. Standing, ripeness, and sovereign immunity significantly limit judicial review at this stage.
