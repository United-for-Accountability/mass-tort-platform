# Investigation Memorandum: The People's Government

**Investigation:** Federal Privatization, Private Control, and the Transfer of Public Authority
**Date opened:** November 18, 2026
**Status:** Pre-Litigation — Evidence Gathering / Legal Review Required
**Prepared for:** Independent attorney review

---

> No complaint has been filed. This memorandum does not state that any agency, official, or company has acted unlawfully. It is a working document to organize the investigation.

## 1. Purpose

This memorandum organizes the factual and legal questions this investigation will pursue. It is a working document that will be revised as records arrive.

## 2. Defining question

When the federal government contracts out functions or infrastructure to private interests, who ultimately controls the public function, and who remains accountable to the people under the Constitution and federal law?

## 3. Scope

The investigation examines:

1. Whether any federal agency, official, or contractor has participated in arrangements that unlawfully transfer governmental authority to private parties.
2. Whether contractors make, or effectively make, final decisions that bind the public or commit federal funds, in violation of the private nondelegation doctrine, the Appointments Clause, or applicable statutes.
3. Who owns the equipment, software, communications systems, and data that essential public functions depend on, and whether that ownership creates accountability or continuity risks.
4. Who ultimately owns and controls the contractors performing essential functions, and whether control is concentrated across agencies in ways that create systemic risk.
5. Whether required statutory notices, records, legal reviews, or approvals were omitted or bypassed.
6. Whether any identifiable person has suffered a concrete, legally cognizable harm traceable to an unlawful arrangement.

The investigation does not presume that privatization is illegal, does not presume a coordinated scheme, and will remain open to evidence that contradicts its starting questions.

## 4. First pilot: FAA air traffic control

The FAA's Brand New Air Traffic Control System modernization program is the first subject because it has identifiable public records: a large, recently funded modernization program ($12.5 billion appropriated July 4, 2025; $50.1 billion requested November 17, 2026), a named prime integrator (Peraton, contract announced December 4, 2025), contractor-run telecommunications networks (L3Harris legacy contract since 2002; AT&T awarded lead role August 11, 2026), a long-standing contract tower program (since 1982, Congress-authorized), recent GAO findings (GAO-26-107992, September 14, 2026), a nationwide ground stop (November 13, 2026), and pending root-cause analysis.

Naming a contract or company in this memorandum records a public fact. It is not an allegation of wrongdoing.

## 7. Legal theories under evaluation

Each theory is currently unproven. They are listed for the purpose of organizing the evidence review.

1. **Private nondelegation** — Whether any contractor exercises final, binding governmental authority without meaningful federal oversight, in violation of the doctrine stated in *Carter v. Carter Coal Co.* (1936) and refined in *FCC v. Consumers' Research* (2025).
2. **Appointments Clause** — Whether any contractor employee exercises significant federal authority without being a properly appointed officer.
3. **Statutory violations** — Whether any agency has contracted out inherently governmental functions in violation of the FAIR Act, OMB Policy Letter 11-01, or applicable agency acquisition regulations.
4. **APA** — Whether any final agency action is arbitrary, capricious, or contrary to law, once a final action and a concrete injury are identified.
5. **FOIA** — Whether records about contracts, ownership, and decisions are being improperly withheld.

## 8. What must be established before any legal action

Before any legal action can be considered, the investigation must establish:

1. A specific, final agency action or decision (not a general policy trend).
2. At least one identifiable plaintiff with a concrete, particularized, and traceable injury.
3. A specific legal violation supported by the record.
4. That the harm is redressable by a court.

None of these conditions has been established as of November 18, 2026.
